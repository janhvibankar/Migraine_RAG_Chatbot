# Over-the-Counter (OTC) Medications for Migraine

> **Disclaimer:** This document is for informational purposes only and is intended to support a RAG-based clinical decision support system. It does not replace professional medical advice. Always consult a licensed healthcare provider before starting, stopping, or changing any medication.

---

## 1. Overview

Over-the-counter medications are the first-line pharmacological treatment for mild-to-moderate migraine attacks. They are available without a prescription and are generally well-tolerated when used as directed. Overuse of OTC analgesics (≥10–15 days/month) can lead to medication-overuse headache (MOH), also called rebound headache.

---

## 2. Simple Analgesics

### 2.1 Acetaminophen (Paracetamol)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Drug Class | Non-opioid analgesic, antipyretic |
| Standard Adult Dose | 500–1000 mg per dose |
| Max Daily Dose | 4000 mg/day (healthy adults); 2000 mg/day (liver disease, alcohol use) |
| Onset of Action | 30–60 minutes |
| Duration | 4–6 hours |
| Route | Oral, rectal (suppository) |

**Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits central prostaglandin synthesis; exact mechanism in migraine not fully established.

**Efficacy in Migraine:**
- Moderately effective for mild-to-moderate migraine.
- 1000 mg acetaminophen shown to provide 2-hour pain relief in ~39–52% of patients vs ~17% placebo.
- Less effective than NSAIDs for severe migraine.

**Safety Profile:**
- Generally safe at recommended doses.
- Hepatotoxic in overdose or with chronic alcohol use.
- Safe in pregnancy (preferred OTC analgesic).
- Can be used in patients with peptic ulcer disease (unlike NSAIDs).

**Contraindications:**
- Severe hepatic impairment
- Known hypersensitivity to acetaminophen
- Chronic heavy alcohol use (relative contraindication)

**MOH Risk:** Moderate — limit to ≤15 days/month.

---

### 2.2 Aspirin (Acetylsalicylic Acid)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Drug Class | NSAID, antiplatelet |
| Standard Adult Dose | 500–1000 mg per dose |
| Max Daily Dose | 4000 mg/day (analgesic use) |
| Onset of Action | 30–60 minutes |
| Duration | 4–6 hours |
| Route | Oral, effervescent |

**Mechanism of Action:** Irreversibly inhibits COX-1 and COX-2, reducing prostaglandin and thromboxane synthesis; also inhibits platelet aggregation.

**Efficacy in Migraine:**
- 1000 mg aspirin shown to be comparable to sumatriptan 50 mg for mild-to-moderate migraine in some studies.
- Effervescent formulations may offer faster absorption.
- Effective for both pain and associated nausea when combined with metoclopramide (prescription).

**Safety Profile:**
- GI irritation, ulceration (especially with long-term use).
- Antiplatelet effects persist for platelet lifetime (~7–10 days).
- Reye's Syndrome risk — **contraindicated in children and adolescents under 16 years** with viral illness.

**Contraindications:**
- Children/adolescents < 16 years (Reye's Syndrome risk)
- Active peptic ulcer disease
- Bleeding disorders or concurrent anticoagulant therapy
- Aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (NSAID-sensitive asthma, nasal polyps)
- Third trimester of pregnancy
- Severe renal or hepatic impairment

**MOH Risk:** Moderate — limit to ≤15 days/month.

## 3. NSAIDs (Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs)

### 3.1 Ibuprofen

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Drug Class | NSAID (propionic acid derivative) |
| Standard Adult Dose | 200–400 mg per dose |
| Max Daily Dose | 1200 mg/day (OTC); 3200 mg/day (prescription supervision) |
| Onset of Action | 30–60 minutes |
| Duration | 4–8 hours |
| Route | Oral, topical |

**Mechanism of Action:** Reversibly inhibits COX-1 and COX-2, reducing prostaglandin synthesis and neurogenic inflammation.

**Efficacy in Migraine:**
- Strong evidence: 400 mg ibuprofen reduces migraine pain significantly at 2 hours vs placebo.
- First-line OTC recommendation in multiple migraine guidelines (AHS, IHS).
- More effective than acetaminophen for moderate-to-severe migraine.

**Safety Profile:**
- GI side effects (dyspepsia, nausea, ulceration); take with food.
- Cardiovascular risk with long-term use.
- Can precipitate acute kidney injury in dehydrated patients (common during migraine attacks).
- Avoid in late pregnancy (third trimester).

**Contraindications:**
- Active GI ulceration or bleeding
- Aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease
- Severe renal, hepatic, or cardiac failure
- Third trimester of pregnancy
- Known hypersensitivity to ibuprofen or other NSAIDs

**MOH Risk:** Moderate — limit to ≤15 days/month.

---

### 3.2 Naproxen Sodium

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Drug Class | NSAID (propionic acid derivative) |
| Standard Adult Dose | 220–440 mg per dose (OTC) |
| Max Daily Dose | 660 mg/day (OTC) |
| Onset of Action | 30–60 minutes |
| Duration | 8–12 hours (longer than ibuprofen) |
| Route | Oral |

**Mechanism of Action:** Reversible COX-1/COX-2 inhibitor; similar to ibuprofen but with longer half-life.

**Efficacy in Migraine:**
- Effective for acute migraine treatment.
- Longer duration of action may reduce migraine recurrence.
- Sometimes used in combination with triptans to prevent headache recurrence.

**Safety Profile:** Similar to ibuprofen; slightly higher cardiovascular risk profile with long-term use compared to ibuprofen.

**Contraindications:** Same as ibuprofen.

**MOH Risk:** Moderate — limit to ≤15 days/month.

---

### 3.3 Diclofenac Potassium

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Drug Class | NSAID (acetic acid derivative) |
| Standard Dose | 50 mg per dose |
| Route | Oral (powder sachet formulation has faster onset) |

**Efficacy:** Diclofenac potassium powder sachet (50 mg) has Level A evidence for acute migraine; rapid absorption leads to faster onset than standard tablets.

## 4. Combination Analgesics

### 4.1 Aspirin + Acetaminophen + Caffeine (e.g., Excedrin Migraine)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Composition | Aspirin 250 mg + Acetaminophen 250 mg + Caffeine 65 mg |
| Standard Adult Dose | 2 tablets per attack (max 2 tablets/24 hours) |
| Route | Oral |

**Mechanism:** Synergistic analgesic effect of aspirin and acetaminophen; caffeine acts as an adjuvant analgesic, enhancing absorption and potency (caffeine adjuvant effect: ~40% increase in analgesia).

**Efficacy in Migraine:**
- Level A evidence from multiple RCTs.
- Significantly more effective than individual components alone.
- Comparable to sumatriptan 50 mg in some head-to-head trials for mild-to-moderate migraine.
- Reduces photophobia, phonophobia, and nausea.

**Safety Profile:**
- Caffeine component can cause dependency; withdrawal headaches with regular use.
- All contraindications of aspirin and acetaminophen apply.
- Not recommended for daily or near-daily use.

**MOH Risk:** HIGH — caffeine-containing combinations carry higher MOH risk; limit strictly to ≤10 days/month.

## 5. Anti-Nausea Agents (OTC)

### 5.1 Dimenhydrinate / Diphenhydramine

- Primarily antiemetic/antihistamine.
- May help nausea associated with migraine.
- Sedating; caution in activities requiring alertness.
- Not a primary migraine analgesic.

### 5.2 Meclizine

- OTC antivertigo/antiemetic agent.
- May help vestibular migraine symptoms.
- Sedating.

---

## 6. Caffeine as an Adjuvant

- Caffeine 100–200 mg alone or in combination can enhance the effect of analgesics.
- Mechanism: Adenosine receptor antagonism; cerebral vasoconstriction; enhanced GI absorption of analgesics.
- Risk: Regular caffeine use leads to dependence; caffeine withdrawal is itself a migraine trigger.

---

## 7. Topical Agents

### 7.1 Peppermint Oil (10% ethanol solution)

- Applied topically to forehead and temples.
- Evidence suggests efficacy comparable to acetaminophen 1000 mg for tension-type headache; some benefit in migraine.
- Mechanism: Menthol activates TRPM8 receptors, producing cooling sensation and inhibiting serotonin receptor activity.
- Safe, minimal systemic side effects.
- Avoid mucous membranes and eyes.

## 8. Medication Overuse Headache (MOH) — Critical Warning

| OTC Medication Type | MOH Threshold |
|---------------------|--------------|
| Simple analgesics (acetaminophen, aspirin, NSAIDs) | ≥ 15 days/month |
| Combination analgesics (with caffeine) | ≥ 10 days/month |
| Opioids (OTC codeine combinations, where available) | ≥ 10 days/month |

**Key Points for RAG System:**
- MOH is the most common cause of chronic daily headache transformation.
- Patients using OTC analgesics >2–3 times/week are at risk.
- MOH resolution typically requires withdrawal of the overused medication (supervised).
- Prevention is better than treatment: early identification of overuse patterns is a core function of a migraine prediction/warning system.

## 10. Special Populations

| Population | Recommendation |
|------------|---------------|
| Pregnancy (1st/2nd trimester) | Acetaminophen preferred; avoid NSAIDs if possible |
| Pregnancy (3rd trimester) | Avoid NSAIDs (risk of premature ductus arteriosus closure); acetaminophen with caution |
| Children (≥12 years) | Ibuprofen 10 mg/kg; acetaminophen 15 mg/kg; aspirin avoided |
| Elderly (>65 years) | Caution with NSAIDs (GI, renal, CV risk); acetaminophen preferred |
| Renal impairment | Avoid NSAIDs; acetaminophen preferred |
| Hepatic impairment | Reduce acetaminophen dose; avoid if severe |
| Peptic ulcer disease | Avoid NSAIDs and aspirin; use acetaminophen |
| Cardiovascular disease | Caution with NSAIDs; naproxen considered lower CV risk among NSAIDs |
