# Medication Side Effects in Migraine Treatment

> **Disclaimer:** This document is for educational and clinical decision support purposes within a RAG-based migraine prediction system. Side effect information is drawn from established clinical literature and prescribing information. It does not replace consultation with a licensed healthcare professional. Patients experiencing adverse effects should contact their healthcare provider.

---

## 1. Overview

Understanding the side effect profiles of migraine medications is critical for:
- Distinguishing medication side effects from migraine symptoms.
- Identifying adverse drug reactions that may themselves trigger migraine.
- Optimizing medication selection based on a patient's comorbidity profile.
- Supporting early warning systems that may detect patterns in side effect reporting.

## 2. OTC Analgesics — Side Effects

### 2.1 Acetaminophen (Paracetamol)

| System | Side Effect | Frequency | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-----------|-------|
| Hepatic | Hepatotoxicity | Rare (at therapeutic doses); common in overdose | Most common cause of acute liver failure (overdose or chronic use with alcohol) |
| Immune | Hypersensitivity reactions (rash, urticaria) | Rare | — |
| Renal | Nephrotoxicity | Rare; risk with long-term high-dose use | Analgesic nephropathy |
| Hematologic | Thrombocytopenia, leukopenia | Very rare | — |

**Overdose Signs:** Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain initially; jaundice, coagulopathy, hepatic failure within 2–3 days.
**N-Acetylcysteine (NAC):** Antidote for acetaminophen overdose.

---

### 2.2 Aspirin

| System | Side Effect | Frequency | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-----------|-------|
| GI | Nausea, dyspepsia, epigastric pain | Common (10–20%) | Take with food; enteric-coated forms reduce upper GI symptoms |
| GI | Peptic ulceration, GI bleeding | Uncommon (therapeutic doses) | Risk increases with age, concurrent NSAIDs, corticosteroids |
| Hematologic | Prolonged bleeding time | Expected pharmacodynamic effect | Irreversible platelet COX-1 inhibition |
| Pulmonary | Aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (AERD) | 10–20% of asthmatics | Bronchoconstriction, rhinitis, urticaria — avoid in NSAID-sensitive asthma |
| Renal | Reduced renal prostaglandins → fluid retention, elevated BP | Uncommon | Risk in volume-depleted patients |
| CNS | Tinnitus, hearing loss | At high doses (salicylism) | Early sign of toxicity |
| Metabolic | Salicylate poisoning | Overdose | Hyperventilation, tinnitus, metabolic acidosis |
| Pediatric | Reye's Syndrome (hepatic encephalopathy) | Rare but serious | Avoid in children <16 with viral illness |

---

### 2.3 Ibuprofen

| System | Side Effect | Frequency | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-----------|-------|
| GI | Dyspepsia, nausea, heartburn | Very common (15–30%) | Take with food or milk; proton pump inhibitor co-prescription for high-risk |
| GI | Peptic ulcer, GI bleeding | Uncommon | Higher risk: elderly, prior peptic ulcer, corticosteroid use |
| Renal | Acute kidney injury | Uncommon; risk elevated during migraine attack (dehydration) | Prostaglandin-dependent renal perfusion blocked; dehydration is a migraine trigger — important interaction |
| Cardiovascular | Hypertension, fluid retention, edema | Common with regular use | COX-2 inhibition shifts to thromboxane A2 dominance |
| Cardiovascular | Increased MI/stroke risk | With long-term regular use | FDA warning; 400 mg occasionally has lower risk than higher doses |
| Hepatic | Elevated liver enzymes | Uncommon | Rarely severe |
| Hematologic | Inhibition of platelet aggregation | Reversible (unlike aspirin) | Returns to normal after clearance |
| Pulmonary | NSAID-sensitive asthma exacerbation | Cross-reactivity with aspirin in AERD | — |
| Skin | Rash, photosensitivity | Uncommon | — |
| CNS | Headache (rebound), dizziness, confusion | Uncommon at standard doses; rebound headache with overuse | Critical for migraine management |

**Migraine-Specific Concern:** Ibuprofen taken ≥15 days/month → medication overuse headache (MOH). Renal prostaglandin blockade during migraine-induced dehydration increases acute kidney injury risk.

---

### 2.4 Naproxen Sodium

Similar to ibuprofen; key differences:
- Longer half-life → longer duration of GI and renal side effects.
- Slightly higher cardiovascular risk than ibuprofen (debated).
- Lower platelet effect per dose, but longer duration of effect.

## 3. Triptan Side Effects

### 3.1 Class Side Effects (All Triptans)

| Side Effect | Frequency | Description |
|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| Triptan sensations | 15–40% | Tingling, numbness, flushing, warmth, heaviness or pressure — particularly in chest, jaw, neck. Generally benign and not cardiac. |
| Chest tightness / chest pain | 5–10% | Usually musculoskeletal or esophageal in etiology; genuine cardiac ischemia rare but possible in predisposed patients |
| Dizziness | 5–15% | — |
| Somnolence / fatigue | 10–20% | — |
| Nausea | 5–10% | Can be attributed to migraine itself |
| Headache recurrence | 20–40% | Headache returns within 24 hours after initial relief; related to triptan half-life |
| Paresthesia | 5–15% | Tingling in limbs |
| Weakness | <5% | — |
| Dry mouth | Common | — |

### 3.2 Serious Adverse Events (Triptans)

| Event | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| Coronary vasospasm / MI | Rare; primarily in patients with undiagnosed CAD — screen patients before prescribing |
| Serotonin syndrome | With concurrent MAOIs, SSRIs, SNRIs, tramadol; presents with agitation, tremor, hyperthermia, clonus |
| Peripheral ischemia | Very rare; theoretical based on vasoconstrictor mechanism |
| Hypertensive crisis | Rare; monitor blood pressure in hypertensive patients |
| Stroke / TIA | Contraindicated in patients with prior cerebrovascular events; independent relationship debated |

### 3.3 Individual Triptan Differences

| Drug | Notable Side Effects |
|------|---------------------|
| Sumatriptan SC | More intense side effects due to rapid peak levels; injection site reactions; most systemic vasoconstriction |
| Sumatriptan nasal | Bad taste, nasal irritation |
| Rizatriptan | Pronounced somnolence; avoid in patients on propranolol (propranolol increases rizatriptan levels — use 5 mg dose) |
| Eletriptan 80 mg | Higher incidence of chest symptoms and somnolence at this dose |
| Frovatriptan | Very low side effect profile due to low peak plasma levels and long half-life |
| Naratriptan | Well tolerated; slow onset; fewer vasospastic side effects |
| Zolmitriptan nasal | Taste disturbance, nasal irritation |

## 4. CGRP Antagonist (Gepant) Side Effects

| Drug | Side Effects | Notes |
|------|-------------|-------|
| Ubrogepant | Nausea (4.5%), somnolence (2%), dry mouth | Generally mild; no significant cardiovascular effects |
| Rimegepant | Nausea (~2%), nasopharyngitis | CYP3A4 interaction important |
| Atogepant | Nausea (~9%), constipation (~8%), fatigue | More frequent at preventive dosing |

**Hepatotoxicity Note:** Earlier gepant class (telcagepant — withdrawn) had hepatotoxicity. Current approved gepants do not show significant hepatotoxicity at approved doses but monitoring is recommended.

| Side Effect | Frequency | Notes |

## 6. Preventive Medication Side Effects

### 6.1 Beta-Blockers (Propranolol, Metoprolol, Timolol, etc.)

| Side Effect | Frequency | Clinical Notes |
|-------------|-----------|---------------|
| Fatigue / decreased exercise tolerance | Very common (20–30%) | Limits use in athletes; may improve with time |
| Bradycardia | Common | Monitor heart rate |
| Hypotension / dizziness | Common | Orthostatic hypotension; risk of falls in elderly |
| Cold extremities | Common | Peripheral vasoconstriction; worsens Raynaud's |
| Depression | 5–10% | Propranolol more so than cardioselective agents |
| Sleep disturbances / vivid dreams | Common with propranolol (lipophilic) | Less with atenolol (hydrophilic) |
| Impotence / sexual dysfunction | 5–10% in men | — |
| Bronchospasm | Uncommon with cardioselective agents; risk with non-selective in asthmatics | Avoid non-selective beta-blockers in asthma/COPD |
| Masking hypoglycemia signs | Expected pharmacodynamic effect | Caution in insulin-dependent diabetes |
| Weight gain | Common | — |
| Rebound hypertension / migraine worsening | If abruptly discontinued | **Taper doses when stopping; never discontinue suddenly** |

---

### 6.2 Topiramate

| Side Effect | Frequency | Clinical Notes |
|-------------|-----------|---------------|
| Cognitive effects ("Dopamax") — word-finding difficulty, memory impairment, slowed thinking | 20–30% | Most concerning side effect; starts at low doses |
| Paresthesia (tingling hands/feet) | Very common (40–50%) | Carbonic anhydrase inhibition → low bicarbonate; often benign |
| Weight loss | Common (5–10% body weight) | Advantage in overweight patients; disadvantage if underweight |
| Appetite suppression | Common | Related to weight loss |
| Somnolence / fatigue | Common | — |
| Metabolic acidosis | Uncommon | Carbonic anhydrase inhibition; check bicarbonate periodically |
| Kidney stones (nephrolithiasis) | 1–2% | Carbonic anhydrase inhibition reduces urinary citrate; increase hydration |
| Acute angle-closure glaucoma | Rare but serious | Onset within days to weeks of starting; ocular pain, vision changes — discontinue immediately |
| Dizziness | Common | — |
| **Teratogenicity** | — | **Category D; neural tube defects, cleft lip/palate; effective contraception mandatory** |
| Mood changes | Uncommon | Depression, irritability |
| Anhidrosis / heat intolerance | Rare, concerning in children | Reduced sweating → overheating risk |

---

### 6.3 Valproate / Sodium Valproate

| Side Effect | Frequency | Clinical Notes |
|-------------|-----------|---------------|
| Nausea / GI upset | Very common initially | Take with food; extended-release forms better tolerated |
| Weight gain | Very common (20–30%) | Significant long-term concern |
| Tremor | Common (10–15%) | Fine tremor; dose-dependent |
| Hair loss (telogen effluvium) | Common (5–10%) | Usually reversible; zinc/selenium supplementation may help |
| Sedation | Common initially | Usually improves |
| Thrombocytopenia | Uncommon | Monitor blood counts |
| Hepatotoxicity | Rare but potentially fatal | Highest risk in children under 2 with metabolic disorders; monitor LFTs |
| Pancreatitis | Rare but serious | Abdominal pain → investigate |
| Hyperammonemia | Uncommon | Can cause confusion/encephalopathy even with normal LFTs |
| Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) | With long-term use | Weight gain + hyperinsulinism mechanism |
| **Teratogenicity** | — | **Highest teratogenic risk among AEDs: neural tube defects (1–2%), cognitive impairment in offspring; ABSOLUTE contraindication in pregnancy unless no alternatives** |
| Menstrual irregularities | Common | — |

---

### 6.4 Amitriptyline / TCAs

| Side Effect | Frequency | Clinical Notes |
|-------------|-----------|---------------|
| Sedation / drowsiness | Very common | Beneficial when taken at night if insomnia is comorbid |
| Dry mouth (xerostomia) | Very common | Dental hygiene concerns with long-term use |
| Constipation | Very common | Anticholinergic effect |
| Urinary retention | Uncommon | Avoid in BPH |
| Blurred vision | Common | Anticholinergic — cycloplegia |
| Tachycardia | Uncommon | — |
| Orthostatic hypotension | Common | Fall risk in elderly; rise slowly from seated/supine |
| Weight gain | Common | Appetite stimulation |
| Cognitive impairment | Uncommon at low migraine doses | More pronounced in elderly (anticholinergic burden) |
| QTc prolongation | Dose-dependent | Risk of arrhythmia, especially in overdose; cardiac monitoring for high-risk patients |
| Serotonin syndrome | With MAOIs, SSRIs | — |
| Lowered seizure threshold | Rare | At high doses |
| **Pregnancy** | — | **Category C; neonatal withdrawal possible; use with caution, especially near delivery** |

### 6.5 CGRP Monoclonal Antibodies

| Drug | Side Effect | Frequency | Notes |
|------|-------------|-----------|-------|
| All SC forms | Injection site reactions (redness, pain, induration) | Common (5–15%) | Usually mild and transient |
| Erenumab | Constipation | 3–4% | CGRP receptor blockade in GI tract; can be severe/worsening |
| All forms | Nasopharyngitis | Common | — |
| All forms | Fatigue | Uncommon | — |
| All forms | Alopecia | Rare case reports | — |
| All forms | Raynaud's phenomenon | Rare case reports | CGRP has vasodilatory function |
| All forms | Hypertension | Rare case reports | CGRP has vasodilatory effects; theoretical |

**Long-term Safety:** Considered excellent; no significant organ toxicity identified in trials to 5 years. Post-marketing surveillance ongoing.

### 6.6 OnabotulinumtoxinA (Botox)

| Side Effect | Frequency | Notes |
|-------------|-----------|-------|
| Neck pain / stiffness | 6–9% | Most common; related to injection into cervical muscles |
| Injection site pain | Common | — |
| Ptosis (eyelid drooping) | 1–4% | Temporary (weeks); local diffusion of toxin |
| Brow drooping | Uncommon | — |
| Eyelid edema | Uncommon | — |
| Dysphagia (swallowing difficulty) | Rare at recommended PREEMPT doses | Risk of spread with high doses or inexperienced injection |
| Local weakness of injected muscles | Expected; temporary | — |
| Flu-like illness | Rare | — |
| Antibody formation (resistance) | Rare with low-frequency use | Higher dose/frequency increases risk |

## 7. Side Effects That May Mimic or Trigger Migraine

This section is especially relevant for a migraine early warning prediction system.

| Medication | Side Effect Resembling/Triggering Migraine | Notes |
|------------|---------------------------------------------|-------|
| Beta-blockers (discontinuation) | Rebound headache, worsening migraine | Never abruptly stop |
| Topiramate | Cognitive dysfunction, fatigue, paresthesia | May be confused with migraine symptoms |
| Calcium channel blockers | Headache (vasodilatory — especially nifedipine) | Paradoxical headache |
| Nitroglycerin / Nitrates | Intense pulsating headache (nitrate-induced migraine) | Well-documented nitric oxide–mediated trigger |
| Phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors (sildenafil, etc.) | Migraine-like headache | NO–mediated mechanism |
| Hormonal contraceptives (estrogen) | Estrogen-withdrawal migraine (pill-free week) | — |
| Caffeine withdrawal | Classic withdrawal headache; significant migraine trigger | Reduction/cessation of caffeine-containing medications or coffee |
| Dipyridamole | Migraine-like headache (vasodilation) | — |
| SSRIs / SNRIs | Can trigger or exacerbate headache initially | Usually transient |
| ACE inhibitors | Cough (alternative: ARBs) | Cough-related headache uncommon but possible |
| MOH from any analgesic | Transformed migraine / chronic daily headache | Most important medication-induced worsening pattern |

## 8. Side Effect Monitoring Recommendations

| Medication | Monitoring Parameter | Frequency |
|------------|---------------------|-----------|
| Valproate | LFTs, CBC, ammonia, drug levels | Every 3–6 months initially |
| Topiramate | Bicarbonate, renal function, weight, mood | Every 3–6 months |
| TCAs | Resting ECG (QTc), blood pressure, weight | Baseline; periodically |
| Beta-blockers | Heart rate, blood pressure, pulmonary function (if asthma risk) | Each visit |
| CGRP mAbs | Blood pressure, constipation (erenumab) | Each visit |
| NSAIDs (chronic) | Renal function, blood pressure, GI symptoms | Every 3–6 months |
| Acetaminophen (daily) | Liver function | Every 6–12 months |

## 9. Side Effects in Special Populations

### Pregnancy
| Medication | Status | Key Concern |
|------------|--------|-------------|
| Valproate | **Absolutely contraindicated** | Highest teratogenicity risk |
| Topiramate | Contraindicated (Category D) | Cleft palate, low birth weight |
| Triptans | Use with caution; sumatriptan has most data | Manufacturer registries show no major signals for sumatriptan |
| NSAIDs | Avoid in 3rd trimester | Premature closure of ductus arteriosus |
| Acetaminophen | Preferred; use minimally | Some recent controversy regarding neurodevelopmental effects at high doses |
| CGRP mAbs | Insufficient data; avoid | IgG crosses placenta |
| Beta-blockers | Use with caution; monitor fetal growth | Neonatal bradycardia, hypoglycemia, growth restriction |
| Magnesium | Generally safe | IV magnesium used in preeclampsia management |

### Elderly
- Higher risk of fall with TCAs, beta-blockers, anticonvulsants (due to orthostatic hypotension, sedation, dizziness).
- Anticholinergic burden of TCAs → cognitive impairment.
- Renal decline → NSAID caution → increased AKI risk.
- Polypharmacy drug interactions more common.

### Pediatric (Adolescents)
- Aspirin contraindicated (<16 years with viral illness).
- Topiramate and amitriptyline commonly used in adolescent migraine prevention.
- Cyproheptadine used in younger children (not adults).
- Valproate in adolescent females: reproductive concerns.

## 10. Management of Common Side Effects

| Side Effect | Management Strategy |
|-------------|---------------------|
| NSAID-related GI upset | Take with food; add proton pump inhibitor (omeprazole, pantoprazole); switch to acetaminophen |
| Triptan sensations (chest tightness, tingling) | Patient education: explain benign nature; if cardiac symptoms → discontinue and evaluate |
| Beta-blocker fatigue | Reduce dose; switch to cardioselective agent; switch drug class |
| Topiramate cognitive effects | Slow dose titration; reduce dose; switch to alternative |
| Topiramate paresthesia | Usually resolves; maintain hydration; no specific treatment needed |
| Valproate weight gain | Dietary counseling; consider switching to topiramate |
| TCA dry mouth | Sugar-free gum/lozenges; pilocarpine (severe cases) |
| TCA sedation | Take at bedtime; reduce dose; switch to nortriptyline (better tolerated) |
| CGRP mAb injection site reactions | Warm injection site; rotate sites; ensure room-temperature injection |
| Erenumab constipation | Dietary fiber; hydration; osmotic laxatives if persistent; consider switching to ligand-targeting mAb |
| Botox ptosis | Reassurance (resolves in weeks); apraclonidine eye drops (sympathomimetic) can help temporarily |
| MOH | Gradual medication withdrawal with physician supervision; bridge therapy; preventive therapy optimization |
