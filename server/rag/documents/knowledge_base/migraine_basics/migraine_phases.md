# Migraine Phases

## Overview

A migraine attack is not simply a headache — it is a complex, multi-phase neurological event that can span 1 to 5 days. Understanding each phase is foundational for early warning and prediction systems, as different phases offer distinct observable signals that can be tracked and used to predict and intervene before severe pain begins.

The four canonical phases are:
1. **Prodrome** (Premonitory Phase)
2. **Aura**
3. **Headache (Ictal) Phase**
4. **Postdrome**

Not all patients experience all four phases; not every attack follows the full sequence. Approximately 20% of migraineurs experience aura; the majority experience prodrome.

---

## Phase 1: Prodrome (Premonitory Phase)

### Timing
- Begins **24–48 hours** before headache onset
- Average duration: **6–10 hours**
- In ~50% of attacks, prodrome lasts <2 hours
- In ~15% of attacks, prodrome lasts 12 hours to 2 days

### Prevalence
- Occurs in the **majority of migraineurs** (estimated 70–80%), though not necessarily before every attack
- Often misattributed to other causes rather than recognized as migraine prodrome

### Symptoms

#### Neurological / Cognitive
- Difficulty concentrating, "brain fog"
- Cognitive slowing
- Reading or writing difficulty ← **highly predictive of imminent headache**
- Speech difficulty ← **highly predictive**
- Depression, irritability, mood changes
- Euphoria or unusual energy

#### Autonomic / Hypothalamic
- Yawning (frequent, repetitive) ← **most characteristic and predictive symptom**
- Food cravings (especially carbohydrates, chocolate, sweet foods)
- Increased thirst and urination
- Fatigue and sleepiness
- Insomnia or altered sleep

#### Sensory
- Increased sensitivity to light (photophobia onset)
- Increased sensitivity to sound (phonophobia onset)
- Hyperosmia (heightened smell sensitivity)
- Neck stiffness or muscle tension ← average 5.0 hours before headache
- Cutaneous allodynia of scalp

#### Gastrointestinal
- Nausea (early)
- Loss of appetite ← average 9.1 hours before headache
- Constipation or diarrhea
- Changes in bowel movement ← average 6.5 hours before headache

### Prodrome Symptom Timing (Average Lead Time Before Headache)
| Symptom | Average Hours Before Headache |
|---|---|
| Loss of appetite | 9.1 hours |
| Sensitivity to smell | 8.5 hours |
| Changes in bowel movement | 6.5 hours |
| Sensitivity to temperature changes | 5.2 hours |
| Muscle tension / neck stiffness | 5.0 hours |
| Yawning | Variable (early, highly predictive) |
| Fatigue | Variable |
| Mood change | Variable |

### Pathophysiology of Prodrome
- Driven primarily by **hypothalamic activation**
- Hypothalamus regulates circadian rhythm, appetite, thirst, and autonomic function — explaining the diverse symptom profile
- Dopaminergic activation accounts for yawning, nausea, and food cravings
- Serotonin fluctuations contribute to mood changes
- Early cortical hyperexcitability manifests as sensory sensitivity

### Clinical Relevance (RAG / Prediction)
- Prodrome is the **primary prediction window** for migraineurs without aura
- Yawning, fatigue, food cravings, and mood changes are the most patient-reportable and trackable symptoms
- Early treatment during prodrome (before headache onset) can reduce or abort the attack
- Triptans or gepants taken in prodrome phase show evidence of attack prevention in some patients
- **Key RAG features:** yawning frequency, mood score, energy level, appetite changes, neck stiffness, sensory sensitivity ratings

## Phase 2: Aura

### Timing
- Occurs after prodrome (if present), typically **20–60 minutes before headache**
- Each aura symptom: **5–60 minutes duration**
- Multiple aura types may occur sequentially

### Prevalence
- Approximately **25–30% of migraineurs** experience aura
- Not present in every attack even in those who have had aura before
- Aura without headache ("silent migraine") is possible

### Types of Aura Symptoms

#### Visual Aura (Most Common — ~90% of auras)
- **Scintillating scotoma:** Central blind spot surrounded by flickering, zigzag crescent of lights
- **Fortification spectra:** Geometric angular pattern resembling fortifications/castle walls
- **Photopsia:** Flashing lights or sparks
- **Blurring of vision**
- Typically spreads outward across visual field over 20–30 minutes

#### Sensory Aura
- Tingling (paresthesia) or numbness
- Classic "cheiro-oral" spread: starts in fingers → hand → arm → face → mouth/tongue
- Spreads gradually (over ≥5 minutes), distinguishing from TIA (sudden onset)

#### Speech/Language Aura
- Dysphasia (difficulty finding words)
- Paraphasia (word substitution errors)
- Slurred speech

#### Motor Aura (Hemiplegic Migraine only)
- Unilateral limb weakness
- Fully reversible

#### Brainstem Aura
- Dysarthria, vertigo, tinnitus, diplopia
- Bilateral visual symptoms
- Decreased level of consciousness

### Pathophysiology of Aura: Cortical Spreading Depression (CSD)

**CSD** is the electrophysiological correlate of migraine aura:
- A slow wave (~2–6 mm/min) of **sustained neuronal and glial depolarization** propagates across the cortex
- Followed by prolonged suppression of electrical activity
- Ion shifts: high extracellular K⁺, high intracellular Na⁺ and Ca²⁺
- Originates in occipital cortex (explaining visual aura); can spread to other cortical regions

**CSD to pain pathway:**
1. CSD initiates in occipital cortex
2. Causes meningeal vasoconstriction then vasodilation
3. Activates trigeminal nerve fibers innervating meninges
4. Triggers CGRP release and neurogenic inflammation
5. Pain signals transmitted to brainstem trigeminal nucleus caudalis
6. Signals relayed to thalamus → cortex → perceived headache

### Clinical Relevance (RAG / Prediction)
- Aura is the **strongest immediate predictor** of oncoming headache (in aura-positive patients)
- Patient self-report of visual disturbances, tingling, or speech changes should trigger immediate alert
- 5–60 minute window exists between aura and headache — critical intervention window
- **Key RAG features:** visual disturbance description, sensory changes (tingling/numbness), speech difficulty, aura duration

## Phase 3: Headache (Ictal) Phase

### Timing
- Follows prodrome and/or aura
- Duration: **4–72 hours** (untreated or inadequately treated)
- Average attack: 12–24 hours

### Characteristics of Headache
- **Location:** Unilateral in ~60% (can switch sides or become bilateral)
- **Quality:** Throbbing, pulsating
- **Intensity:** Moderate to severe
- **Aggravation:** Physical activity (walking, climbing stairs)
- **Associated symptoms:**
  - Nausea (80% of patients)
  - Vomiting (50%)
  - Photophobia (80%)
  - Phonophobia (76%)
  - Osmophobia (40%)
  - Cutaneous allodynia (scalp tenderness in 70% during attack)

### Pain Severity Scale
| Severity | Description |
|---|---|
| Mild (1–3/10) | Aware of headache; can continue activity |
| Moderate (4–6/10) | Headache inhibits but doesn't prohibit daily activity |
| Severe (7–10/10) | Unable to perform daily activities; bed rest required |

### Pathophysiology of Headache Phase

#### Trigeminovascular Pathway Activation
1. Trigeminal nerve fibers (Aδ and C fibers) innervate pain-sensitive structures: meningeal blood vessels, dura mater, large cerebral arteries
2. Activation triggers release of:
   - **CGRP** (key vasodilator; causes meningeal vessel dilation)
   - **Substance P**
   - **Neurokinin A**
3. These mediators cause: vasodilation, plasma extravasation, mast cell degranulation → **neurogenic inflammation**
4. Signals travel via trigeminal ganglion → **trigeminal nucleus caudalis (TNC)** in brainstem
5. Second-order neurons project to **thalamus**
6. Third-order neurons project to **somatosensory cortex** — pain is perceived

#### Central Sensitization
- Ongoing nociceptive input leads to sensitization of second-order neurons in TNC
- Manifests as **cephalic allodynia** (scalp pain from light touch/brushing hair)
- Further sensitization of third-order thalamic neurons → **extracephalic allodynia** (whole-body sensitivity)
- Central sensitization is why triptans are less effective when taken late in the headache phase

#### CGRP Elevation
- Plasma CGRP levels are markedly elevated during migraine attacks
- Return to baseline after successful triptan treatment
- CGRP is both a biomarker and a therapeutic target (gepants, CGRP mAbs)

### Brainstem Structures Involved
- **Trigeminal nucleus caudalis (TNC):** First relay for pain signals; in medulla
- **Locus coeruleus:** Noradrenergic; involved in sensory amplification
- **Dorsal raphe nucleus:** Serotonergic; dysfunction during migraine
- **Periaqueductal gray (PAG):** Descending pain modulation; may be dysfunctional in migraine, allowing pain to amplify

### Autonomic Features During Headache
- Nausea and vomiting (vagal activation)
- Pallor, cold extremities
- Nasal congestion or rhinorrhea (rare)
- Ptosis or lid edema (in some)

## Phase 4: Postdrome

### Timing
- Begins after headache resolution
- Duration: **hours to 48 hours** (average ~24 hours)
- Often called the "migraine hangover"

### Prevalence
- Experienced by **~68–80% of migraineurs** after attacks

### Symptoms

#### Cognitive / Neurological
- Cognitive impairment ("brain fog")
- Difficulty concentrating
- Difficulty with memory
- Fatigue and exhaustion (most common symptom)
- Weakness

#### Mood
- Depression, low mood
- Irritability
- Euphoria or emotional sensitivity (less common)

#### Sensory
- Residual photophobia or phonophobia
- Scalp tenderness (residual allodynia)

#### Physical
- Muscle weakness
- Stiff neck
- Decreased appetite
- Nausea (residual)

### Pathophysiology of Postdrome
- Not fully understood
- Neuroimaging (PET/fMRI) shows **reduced cortical blood flow and cerebral activity** after attack
- Persistent altered dopaminergic and serotonergic tone
- Residual central sensitization
- Hypothalamic dysregulation continuing beyond headache

### Clinical Relevance (RAG / Prediction)
- Postdrome symptoms can overlap with early prodrome of the next attack
- Tracking postdrome duration helps predict inter-attack intervals
- Fatigue and cognitive impairment scores are trackable features
- **Key RAG features:** fatigue rating, cognitive performance, residual sensitivity, mood state

## Early Warning Symptoms Most Predictive of Imminent Headache

Based on clinical research, the following prodrome symptoms are most strongly predictive of an imminent migraine headache:

1. **Yawning** (most characteristic, highly specific to migraine prodrome)
2. **Difficulty with speech, reading, or writing** (high specificity)
3. **Blurred vision** (during prodrome, distinct from aura)
4. **Food cravings**
5. **Neck stiffness/muscle tension**
6. **Unusual fatigue**
7. **Mood changes (irritability, depression, or unusual euphoria)**
8. **Increased urination**
9. **Sensory hypersensitivity** (photophobia, phonophobia, osmophobia)

## Intervention Windows

| Window | Phase | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| 24–48h before | Early prodrome | Log symptoms; modify behavior; consider preventive dose |
| 6–12h before | Late prodrome | Take acute medication (early intervention) |
| 5–60 min before | Aura | Take triptan or gepant immediately |
| At pain onset | Headache (mild) | Take acute medication before central sensitization |
| During severe headache | Headache (severe) | Rescue medication; may need parenteral treatment |

## Sleep and Phase Disruption

- Disrupted sleep is both a trigger and a consequence of migraine phases
- Sleep can abort a migraine attack (migraine-specific sleep response)
- Oversleeping ("weekend migraine") can also trigger attacks
- Circadian rhythm disruption worsens prodrome symptoms
- Sleep diary integration is valuable for prediction models
