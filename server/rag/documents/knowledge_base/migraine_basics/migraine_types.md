# Migraine Types

## Overview

Migraine is not a single uniform disorder but a spectrum of neurological conditions sharing core mechanisms yet differing in presentation, triggers, severity, and frequency. The International Classification of Headache Disorders (ICHD-3) provides the standard taxonomy.

---

## Primary Classification

### 1. Migraine Without Aura (Common Migraine)
**ICHD-3 Code: 1.1**

- Most prevalent type — accounts for **~75% of all migraine cases**
- No neurological warning symptoms before headache
- Headache: unilateral, pulsating, moderate-to-severe, lasting 4–72 hours
- Associated with nausea, vomiting, photophobia, phonophobia
- Worsened by routine physical activity
- Diagnosis requires ≥5 attacks meeting criteria

**Relevance for prediction:** Harder to predict than migraine with aura since there is no clear aura signal; prodrome symptoms become the primary early warning indicator.

---

### 2. Migraine With Aura (Classic Migraine)
**ICHD-3 Code: 1.2**

- Accounts for **~25–30% of migraines**
- Characterized by fully reversible neurological symptoms (aura) preceding headache by 20–60 minutes
- Aura develops gradually over ≥5 minutes and lasts 5–60 minutes per symptom
- Multiple aura symptoms may occur sequentially

#### Aura Subtypes

| Aura Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Visual aura | Most common (~90% of auras); zigzag lines (fortification spectra), scotoma, flickering lights, blurred vision |
| Sensory aura | Tingling or numbness, typically starting in hand and spreading to face (cheiro-oral distribution) |
| Speech/language aura | Dysphasia or difficulty finding words |
| Motor aura | Weakness (see Hemiplegic Migraine) |
| Brainstem aura | Dysarthria, vertigo, tinnitus, diplopia, ataxia |
| Retinal aura | Monocular visual disturbances |

**Typical visual aura:** Scintillating scotoma starting centrally, expanding as a crescent of flickering zigzag lines, leaving a blind spot; lasts 20–30 minutes.

**Pathophysiology:** Aura is produced by cortical spreading depression (CSD) — a slow (2–6 mm/min) wave of neuronal and glial depolarization spreading across the cortex, followed by suppressed activity.

**Relevance for prediction:** Aura is itself a strong predictor of imminent headache; automated detection of aura-like symptoms via patient input can trigger early intervention.

---

### 3. Chronic Migraine
**ICHD-3 Code: 1.3**

- **Definition:** ≥15 headache days per month for >3 consecutive months, of which ≥8 are migraine days
- Prevalence: ~2–3% of general population; ~8% of migraine sufferers
- Often evolves from episodic migraine through a process of **chronification**

#### Risk Factors for Chronification
- Medication overuse (analgesics, triptans >10–15 days/month)
- Obesity (BMI >30 increases risk ~5×)
- Sleep disorders
- High attack frequency at baseline
- Anxiety and depression
- Stressful life events
- Female sex

#### Management
- Preventive therapy is mandatory: CGRP monoclonal antibodies, topiramate, valproate, amitriptyline
- Botulinum toxin (onabotulinumtoxin A) — FDA-approved specifically for chronic migraine
- Withdrawal of overused medications

---

### 4. Episodic Migraine
- **Definition:** <15 headache days per month
- Subdivided into:
  - **Low-frequency episodic:** <10 days/month
  - **High-frequency episodic:** 10–14 days/month (higher risk of chronification)
- Standard acute treatment approach

---

## Specialized Migraine Types

### 5. Hemiplegic Migraine
**ICHD-3 Codes: 1.2.3 (Familial), 1.2.4 (Sporadic)**

- Rare; characterized by **motor weakness (hemiplegia)** as part of the aura
- Motor weakness must be fully reversible
- Can last hours to days
- Other aura features (visual, sensory, speech) co-occur

**Genetic subtypes (Familial Hemiplegic Migraine - FHM):**
| Subtype | Gene | Protein |
|---|---|---|
| FHM1 | CACNA1A | P/Q-type calcium channel |
| FHM2 | ATP1A2 | Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase |
| FHM3 | SCN1A | Voltage-gated sodium channel |

- Emergency considerations: distinguish from stroke/TIA

---

### 6. Vestibular Migraine (Migraine-Associated Vertigo)
**ICHD-3 Code: A1.6.5**

- Characterized by episodic vestibular symptoms (vertigo, dizziness, imbalance) associated with migraine
- Vertigo episodes last 5 minutes to 72 hours
- One of the most common causes of episodic vertigo in adults
- May occur with or without headache during the episode
- Diagnosis requires history of migraine AND current vestibular symptoms

---

### 7. Menstrual Migraine
**ICHD-3 Code: A1.1.1 / A1.2.0.1**

- Triggered by the drop in estrogen just before menstruation
- **Pure menstrual migraine:** Occurs exclusively during menstruation (within 2 days before to 3 days after onset), in at least 2 of 3 cycles
- **Menstrually related migraine:** Occurs during menstruation but also at other times
- Often more severe, longer-lasting, and more resistant to treatment than non-menstrual attacks
- Management: perimenstrual preventive therapy (frovatriptan, naproxen, transdermal estrogen)

---

### 8. Retinal Migraine (Ocular Migraine)
**ICHD-3 Code: 1.2.6**

- Repeated attacks of monocular (one eye) visual disturbance including scintillations, scotoma, or blindness
- Associated with ipsilateral headache
- Reversible within 60 minutes
- Rare; important to distinguish from amaurosis fugax (TIA) or retinal artery occlusion

---

### 9. Silent Migraine (Acephalgic Migraine / Migraine Without Headache)
- Aura symptoms occur **without headache**
- Visual disturbances, sensory changes, speech difficulties, or other aura symptoms present
- More common in older adults; may represent evolution of typical migraine
- Important: first-time aura without headache must be evaluated to rule out TIA

---

### 10. Abdominal Migraine (Pediatric)
**ICHD-3 Code: 1.6.1**

- Primarily affects children (aged 5–12)
- Recurrent episodes of moderate-to-severe midline abdominal pain lasting 2–72 hours
- Associated with nausea, vomiting, pallor, anorexia
- No headache required for diagnosis
- Strong family history of migraine
- Often precedes development of typical migraine in adulthood
- Part of **migraine equivalents** or **childhood periodic syndromes**

---

### 11. Status Migrainosus
**ICHD-3 Code: 1.4.1**

- Debilitating migraine attack lasting **>72 hours**
- Continuous or with headache-free intervals <4 hours
- Requires medical intervention
- Risk factors: overuse of acute medications, high stress, hormonal changes
- Treatment: IV fluids, IV antiemetics (prochlorperazine, metoclopramide), IV magnesium, IV steroids, DHE (dihydroergotamine) protocols

---

### 12. Chronic Daily Headache (CDH) Overlap
- Umbrella term including chronic migraine, chronic tension-type headache, and hemicrania continua
- Medication overuse headache (MOH) is a major contributor in CDH patients

---

## Migraine Equivalents / Precursor Syndromes (Childhood)

| Syndrome | Description |
|---|---|
| Cyclical vomiting syndrome | Recurrent stereotyped episodes of vomiting |
| Abdominal migraine | Episodic midline abdominal pain |
| Benign paroxysmal vertigo of childhood | Brief vertigo episodes without headache |
| Benign paroxysmal torticollis | Head tilt episodes, may be vestibular migraine precursor |

---

## Migraine with Brainstem Aura (Basilar Migraine)
**ICHD-3 Code: 1.2.2**

- Aura symptoms originating from brainstem or bilateral occipital cortex
- Symptoms: dysarthria, vertigo, tinnitus, diplopia, bilateral visual changes, bilateral paresthesias, ataxia, decreased consciousness
- Not explained by hemiplegic migraine
- Must have ≥2 brainstem aura symptoms

## Summary Classification Table

| Type | Prevalence | Key Distinguishing Feature | Prediction Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Without aura | ~75% | Headache without warning neurological symptoms | Prodrome symptoms only |
| With aura | ~25% | Visual/sensory/speech neurological warning | Aura + prodrome |
| Chronic | ~2–3% | ≥15 headache days/month | High baseline frequency |
| Hemiplegic | Rare | Motor weakness aura; genetic | Motor weakness + aura |
| Vestibular | ~1% | Episodic vertigo | Vertigo episodes |
| Menstrual | ~14% of women | Tied to menstrual cycle | Hormonal cycle tracking |
| Retinal | Very rare | Monocular visual loss | Visual mono symptoms |
| Silent | Underdiagnosed | Aura without headache | Isolated aura |
| Abdominal | Children | Abdominal pain, no headache | GI symptoms in kids |
| Status migrainosus | Uncommon | >72 hours continuous | Duration tracking |

---

## Clinical Relevance for RAG / Prediction System

- **Type identification** is critical: different types have different predictive features
- Menstrual migraine → hormonal cycle as input feature
- Vestibular migraine → balance/dizziness as early signal
- Migraine with aura → visual disturbance logging as prediction input
- Chronic migraine → frequency tracking and risk of MOH
- Type affects treatment selection, urgency, and preventive strategy
