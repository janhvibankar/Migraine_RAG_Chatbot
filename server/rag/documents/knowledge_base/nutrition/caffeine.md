## 1. Overview: Caffeine and Migraine — A Dual Role

Caffeine is uniquely bidirectional in migraine: it can both **relieve** and **trigger** attacks, depending on dose, timing, frequency of use, and individual sensitivity. It is the most widely consumed psychoactive substance globally, and its relationship with migraine is complex and clinically significant.

**Core clinical facts:**
- Caffeine is present in 58+ plants including coffee, tea, cacao, guarana, yerba mate
- Caffeine withdrawal headache is a formally recognized disorder (ICHD-3: 8.3.3)
- Low-dose caffeine (< 200 mg) potentiates analgesics by 40%; hence present in Excedrin, Fioricet
- Habitual caffeine intake > 200 mg/day is associated with increased migraine frequency
- Caffeine overuse is a significant risk factor for chronic daily headache (CDH) transformation
- Approximately 50% of chronic migraine patients have caffeine use disorder patterns

## 2. Pharmacology of Caffeine

### Mechanism of Action
- **Primary:** Competitive antagonism of adenosine receptors (A1 and A2A)
- Adenosine normally causes sedation, vasodilation, and pain modulation; caffeine blocks these effects
- In migraine: adenosine causes cerebral vasodilation (pain-producing); caffeine-induced vasoconstriction can abort early migraine
- **Secondary:** Increases dopamine and norepinephrine release → mood elevation, alertness
- **Tertiary:** Inhibits phosphodiesterase → increases cAMP → enhanced neurotransmitter effect

### Pharmacokinetics
| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Oral bioavailability | ~100% |
| Time to peak plasma | 30–60 minutes |
| Half-life | 3–7 hours (average 5 hours) |
| Half-life range | 1.5–9.5 hours (genetic variation, liver function, smoking, pregnancy) |
| Liver metabolism | CYP1A2 enzyme |
| Active metabolite | Paraxanthine (75%), theobromine, theophylline |
| Protein binding | 35% |

### CYP1A2 Genetic Variants and Migraine Relevance
- **Fast metabolizers (CYP1A2\*1F):** Process caffeine quickly; may need higher doses for effect; lower withdrawal risk but higher tolerance buildup
- **Slow metabolizers:** Caffeine persists longer; even moderate doses can impair sleep; higher cardiovascular and migraine risk from caffeine
- Smoking induces CYP1A2 → smokers metabolize caffeine ~50% faster

## 4. Caffeine as Migraine Trigger

### Acute Trigger Scenarios
1. **High single-dose intake:** > 400–500 mg in a single session → overstimulation → rebound vasodilation after metabolism → headache
2. **Caffeine after period of abstinence:** Even 1 cup after 2–3 days abstinence can trigger rebound headache in habituated users
3. **Energy drinks:** Combined caffeine + taurine + niacin (niacin flush → vasodilation) + high sugar → multi-mechanism trigger
4. **Late-day caffeine:** Impairs sleep → sleep deprivation → next-day migraine (caffeine after 2 PM for 10 PM sleepers)

### Caffeine Withdrawal Headache (ICHD-3: 8.3.3)
**Diagnostic criteria:**
- Regular caffeine use > 200 mg/day for > 2 weeks
- Headache develops within 24 hours after last caffeine intake
- Headache resolves within 1 hour of 100 mg caffeine intake, OR resolves within 7 days of abstinence
- Character: bilateral, pulsating, worsened by physical activity

**Mechanism:**
- Habitual caffeine use upregulates adenosine receptors (compensatory)
- Upon caffeine withdrawal: adenosine floods upregulated receptors → massive cerebral vasodilation → throbbing headache
- Timeline: Symptoms begin 12–24 hours after last dose; peak 20–51 hours; resolve 2–9 days

**Withdrawal Severity by Daily Dose:**
| Daily Caffeine Intake | Withdrawal Headache Severity | Duration |
|----------------------|-----------------------------|-----------| 
| 100–200 mg | Mild | 1–2 days |
| 200–400 mg | Moderate | 2–4 days |
| > 400 mg | Severe | 4–9 days |

**Situations Precipitating Withdrawal Migraine:**
- Weekend lie-in (delays morning coffee by 1–3 hours)
- Fasting/skipped breakfast (delays caffeine intake)
- Illness, nausea preventing coffee consumption
- Deliberate caffeine reduction without tapering
- Hospitalization, NPO (nil per os) status
- Travel across time zones affecting routine

**RAG Predictive Signals:**
- "I didn't have my morning coffee yet / later than usual" → Withdrawal headache risk HIGH
- "I'm trying to quit caffeine" → Withdrawal migraine risk; taper guidance needed
- "I didn't sleep and skipped coffee" → Compound risk (sleep deprivation + withdrawal)

## 5. Caffeine as Migraine Treatment

### Acute Analgesic Potentiation
- Caffeine 65–100 mg co-administered with aspirin or acetaminophen increases analgesic efficacy by **40%**
- Mechanism: adenosine antagonism reduces pain signaling; vasoconstrictive effect counters migraine vasodilation
- Number Needed to Treat (NNT) for aspirin + caffeine vs. aspirin alone: 14 → 8.6
- This is the basis of **Excedrin Migraine** (aspirin 250mg + acetaminophen 250mg + caffeine 65mg)

### Caffeine Dosing for Acute Migraine
- Effective dose range: 65–200 mg at attack onset
- Must be used ≤ 2 days/week to avoid medication overuse headache (MOH)
- Ineffective or harmful if: used after 48-hour mark of attack, or if caffeine withdrawal is actually the cause of headache

### Triptans and Caffeine Interaction
- Caffeine does not potentiate triptans and should not replace them
- Combining caffeine with vasoconstrictive triptans (e.g., sumatriptan) is generally safe at therapeutic doses
- Avoid high caffeine doses (> 200 mg) combined with ergotamine derivatives (additive vasospasm risk)

## 6. Chronic Caffeine Use and Migraine Chronification

### The Medication Overuse Headache (MOH) Risk
- Caffeine is an independent risk factor for MOH when consumed in analgesic preparations > 10 days/month
- Regular caffeine consumption sensitizes central pain pathways
- Daily or near-daily caffeine creates a "dependency cycle": intake → short-term relief → withdrawal → headache → more caffeine
- Patients with > 5 caffeine-containing analgesic days/week have 3× higher risk of chronic daily headache

### Dose-Frequency Thresholds
| Caffeine Pattern | Migraine Risk |
|-----------------|---------------|
| 0–50 mg/day (occasional) | Very Low |
| 50–200 mg/day (1–2 cups coffee) | Low-Moderate |
| 200–400 mg/day (3–4 cups) | Moderate; sleep impairment likely |
| > 400 mg/day | HIGH; MOH risk; chronic migraine risk |
| Variable/erratic intake | HIGH regardless of mean dose (withdrawal cycles) |
| Consistent daily intake at same time | Lower risk than erratic pattern of same dose |

### Key Principle: **Consistency is More Protective Than Amount**
- A migraineur who drinks 2 cups at exactly the same time every day has LOWER risk than one who drinks erratically
- Timing variability (> 1 hour from usual caffeine intake time) is a stronger predictor of caffeine-withdrawal headache than dose alone

## 7. Caffeine Reduction / Cessation Protocol

### Tapering (to Minimize Withdrawal Migraine)
**Recommended reduction: 10–25 mg per week**
- Example for 400 mg/day → 0:
  - Week 1: 375 mg/day
  - Week 2: 350 mg/day
  - Week 3: 300 mg/day
  - (Continue until 0 over 8–16 weeks)
- Alternative: Switch from coffee to tea (natural dose reduction) → green tea → herbal tea
- Reduce concentration gradually (dilute coffee, switch to half-caf)

### Managing Withdrawal Symptoms
- Adequate hydration during taper (withdrawal increases dehydration risk)
- NSAIDs (ibuprofen 400 mg) can blunt withdrawal headache during taper
- Schedule reduction during low-stress periods
- Maintain sleep schedule strictly during taper

## 8. Caffeine Sensitivity Factors

### Factors Increasing Caffeine Sensitivity (Higher Migraine Risk)
- Female sex (lower lean body mass → higher plasma concentration per mg)
- Oral contraceptive use (OCP doubles caffeine half-life via CYP1A2 inhibition)
- Pregnancy (caffeine half-life increases to 15+ hours in third trimester)
- Liver disease (reduced CYP1A2 activity)
- Slow CYP1A2 genotype
- Anxiety disorder (caffeine worsens anxious arousal)
- Low body weight
- No prior caffeine exposure / infrequent use

### Factors Decreasing Caffeine Sensitivity (Lower Risk Per Dose)
- Tobacco smoking (CYP1A2 induction → faster metabolism)
- High caffeine habituation (receptor downregulation)
- High body mass
- Fast CYP1A2 genotype

## 9. Caffeine and Sleep — Migraine Amplification Loop

- Caffeine within 6 hours of bedtime reduces total sleep time by ~45 minutes (objective PSG data)
- Reduces slow-wave (N3) sleep by ~20%
- Even if subjectively sleep feels normal, sleep quality is impaired
- Poor sleep → next-day migraine risk → patient takes more caffeine to combat fatigue → cycle continues

### The Fatigue-Caffeine-Migraine Loop
```
Poor sleep → fatigue → increased caffeine intake → impaired next-night sleep
    ↑                                                           ↓
Migraine ←————— sleep deprivation + caffeine withdrawal ←————————
```

**RAG Predictive Signal:**
- Late afternoon/evening coffee reported → Sleep disruption + dehydration + next-day migraine risk

## 10. Special Populations

### Pediatric/Adolescent Migraineurs
- Recommended caffeine limit: < 100 mg/day for ages 12–18
- Energy drink consumption in adolescents strongly associated with migraine attacks
- Caffeine withdrawal headache pattern common on school holidays (delayed morning routine)

### Menstrual Migraine
- Perimenstrual estrogen drop + caffeine use compounds migraine risk
- Caffeine withdrawal during menstrual days (nausea → can't drink coffee) → withdrawal headache
- Consistent low-dose caffeine throughout cycle is safer than variable use

### Migraine with Aura
- Higher cardiovascular risk with caffeine + oral contraceptives in migraine with aura patients
- Caffeine + ergot combinations are contraindicated due to additive vasospasm risk

### High-Priority Caffeine Trigger Phrases
- "I missed my morning coffee / had it late today" → Withdrawal risk HIGH
- "I had a lot of coffee to power through work" → Rebound + sleep disruption risk
- "I stopped drinking coffee recently" → Withdrawal headache risk
- "I had energy drinks" → HIGH risk (caffeine + additives)
- "I took Excedrin / Fioricet today" → Caffeine exposure; count toward weekly limit
- "I've been drinking coffee all day" → > 400 mg threshold; chronic risk
- "I'm cutting back on caffeine" → Ensure tapering; withdrawal flag
